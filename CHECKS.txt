PLAY CHECKS OWED  (17 checks, 3 loads, about 65 minutes)

Answer on the line that starts with ">". Leave it blank if you didn't try that one.
Write whatever you like: "yes", "no, it did X", "didn't try". Prose is fine.
Cheats (debug builds): on the board [1] adds $500, [2] adds 20 rep, [4] takes 20 rep off,
[3] skips to Friday's payday; in a job [0] finishes it perfectly.


================================================================================
LOAD 1 of 2: A JOB ON FOOT      (about 15 min, a squirrel hater with a hedge is best)
================================================================================

S13-WALL
For a customer who wants squirrels gone: throw a squirrel hard into the house wall while
they watch. No "Careful!"; the knockout makes them laugh.
>

S13-HEDGE
Stand a few steps back from a hedge (not right against it) and throw a squirrel over at
full power while a hater watches: it lands next door and they're glad. From right against
the hedge it bounces back. Does clearing a hedge feel fair?
>

S13-KILL
Knock a critter out with a stone, then hit it again while it's out cold (another stone,
or throw it hard at a wall): it dies and lies there as a body.
>

S13-STONE
A stone that hits a critter drops beside it instead of vanishing.
>

S13-PATIO
Drag the hose onto the paving in front of the house: it lies on top of the slabs.
>

S13-POCKETS
Get paid, then knock the customer out and rifle them: only small change. (Knock one out
before paying you, for comparison, if you like.)
>


================================================================================
LOAD 2 of 2: THE MOWERS      (about 10 min; [1] on the board for money, buy petrol and ride-on)
================================================================================

S13-CORD
Start the petrol mower: a hand grips the cord on the bar and draws it as you hold; let go
in the green and it yanks away as the engine starts. Does it read now?
>

S13-RIDEDRY
Run the ride-on dry: it won't move or turn, and the hint says fetch a can. Carry a petrol
can to it, refuel, key it on.
>

S13-IDLE
With the petrol mower running, stand still for a while, then drive: the fuel bar falls
noticeably slower standing than moving.
>


================================================================================
LOAD 3 of 3: THE SEASON PROTOTYPE      (about 25 min; New business on the title)
================================================================================

S14-CAL
New business: the board shows Tuesday 1 April 1980 and April's calendar. Ring an ad, Go,
play it, and after the summary the board is on Wednesday with the job gone from the grid.
Is the board readable at a glance (calendar, today, the paper, the shop)?
>

S14-RING
Ring the paper's ads top to bottom. The big ones mostly say "Oh, I've heard of you. No."
and stay stamped; now and then one a little above you says "go on, then". Does the phone
call make your reputation feel real, and is the reach above your level about right?
>

S14-PAYDAY
[3] to Friday: payday names the vig and your keep. Pay extra off the debt; after, the
header's "Owed the shark" and next Friday's figure are both lower.
>

S14-OFFER
Finish a classifieds job perfectly ([0] in the job): the summary may bring a regular's
offer (a hidden chance, likelier from a busy customer). Try the haggle. Did one come?
>

S14-VISIT
Play a regular's visit (green on the calendar; step through with "On to the next job",
since [3] skips booked days too): the same garden, "Back again" in the briefing, a gnome
new since the last visit from the second one on, no tip however well it goes.
>

S14-COURT
Knock a customer out so the police are called. Once caught: "court in the morning", the
court screen, a lawyer, a verdict. Once escaped: a summons, court a few days on. Guilty
of assault means community service ("Duty" on the calendar): the churchyard, unpaid, a
reputation line for it. Do the sentences feel proportionate?
>

S14-BLACKOUT
Start a job, pause, Quit to the title, then Continue: "YOU BLACKED OUT", the job gone.
>

S14-PACE
Overall: does a week feel right, with the skip through empty days, one to three ads a
week saying yes, and $150 due every Friday? Too tight, too loose, too many clicks?
>
